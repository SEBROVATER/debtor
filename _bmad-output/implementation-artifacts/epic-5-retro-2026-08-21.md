### What Went Well

- Exact financial correctness was kept central: one complete snapshot precedes provider I/O, calculations use checked `Decimal`, quantization preserves a zero sum, and settlement operates only on complete results.
- Safety boundaries were strengthened: archival captures immutable ledger/date/quote context and revalidates it under the shared write gate; restore remains intentionally independent of rate and balance eligibility.
- Review feedback produced concrete corrections before closure, including stale-evidence validation, no-partial financial errors, scoped enhanced failures, lifecycle feedback, and archive admission invariants.
- Native server-rendered paths remained authoritative while the HTMX path stayed constrained to equivalent server-rendered behavior.

### Challenges And Lessons

- Similar presentation and interaction defects appeared across multiple stories: enhanced failure replacement, focus retention, status announcements, and parity between native and enhanced paths. These should be designed and tested as acceptance criteria from the start rather than found late in review.
- Lifecycle features required careful separation of related concepts. Archive must be based on a new immutable Historical calculation and final admission checks; restore must change only archive state after lifecycle and ownership checks.
- A pre-existing `saturating_sub` remains in `debtor-domain/src/debts/simplify.rs`. It conflicts with the checked-arithmetic accounting rule even though the surrounding settlement invariants are covered.
